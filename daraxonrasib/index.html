Daraxonrasib explained

A five-minute narrated explainer on the pan-RAS drug for pancreatic cancer, and the genetics, maths and AI tools that could help repeat it · November 2026

Interactive version (animation in the browser) Download MP4 (11 MB) FDA approval announcement

What it covers

  1. The accelerator. RAS is a growth switch; a mutation jams it on. More than nine in ten pancreatic cancers carry one.
  2. Why it was "undruggable". RAS is small and smooth. Daraxonrasib first binds a helper protein, cyclophilin A, and the pair clamps onto active RAS.
  3. The evidence. In the RASolute 302 phase 3 trial, median survival was 13.2 months versus 6.7 months on chemotherapy. FDA approval followed on 26 August 2026.
  4. Honest limits. It is not a cure; side effects and resistance are real.
  5. Beyond the pancreas. Lung and bowel cancer studies and basket trials.
  6. The pipeline. The drug came from classic chemistry, not AI, but sequencing, AlphaFold, protein design, smarter trials, liquid biopsy and DNA language models could make the next one faster.
