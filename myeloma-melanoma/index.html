Myeloma and melanoma explained

An eight-minute narrated explainer on living with two cancers at once, made for a friend in his seventies · November 2026

Interactive version (animation in the browser) Download MP4 (17 MB)

What it covers

  1. Myeloma. A blood cancer of plasma cells in the bone marrow, now often managed for years with triple therapy such as daratumumab, lenalidomide and dexamethasone.
  2. Melanoma surgery and staging. Wide excision, the sentinel node, and how thickness, ulceration and node involvement set the stage.
  3. Fifteen years of progress. Checkpoint immunotherapy and BRAF/MEK tablets transformed melanoma care from 2011, including treatment after surgery.
  4. Shared wiring. The RAS–BRAF–MEK–ERK relay is often faulty in both cancers.
  5. Having both. Infection risk, how the drugs combine, steroids, and why the two teams need to plan together.
  6. Questions and trials. What to ask the oncologist and haematologist, and trials to watch, such as the personalised mRNA vaccine intismeran.
